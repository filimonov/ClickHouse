Additional write classes or materially omitted subpaths:

- **Repeated physical blob PUT/copy.** A concrete inferred schedule is: upload A applies but loses its response; GC1 delays `delete(A)`; GC2 deletes A and its marker; a live writer publishes B; the zombie’s SDK retries A; GC1’s old delete now matches A again. Today’s fresh-envelope rule covers outer invocations, not these retries. Fix: prevent identical-envelope reapplication in every publication layer; each permissible new publication must mint a fresh incarnation.
- **Administrative owner retirement and control-object deletion.** `Tools/CasDecommission.cpp:decommissionPoolMember` deletes mount/counter objects, then CAS-tombstones an owner that same-UUID startup does not rewrite. Its comments explicitly acknowledge successor tombstoning. Deletion also permits epoch reminting during the retirement gap. Today’s protection is exact deletion plus separate rechecks, with an accepted race. Fix: retain the monotone counter and serialize retirement against claims through one durable authority.
- **Delayed startup staging sweep.** Today it performs an unfenced broad-prefix list/delete after claim. Fix without changing object format: freeze deletion candidates **before claim**, then delete only those unique keys after winning; alternatively use epoch-scoped staging.
- **GC marker deletion**, `Gc/CasGcMetaWriter.cpp:deleteConfirmedMeta`, was omitted from the inventory. Round checks and exact marker tokens protect newer condemnations; after fixing Z6 and publication reincarnation, no additional clock mechanism is needed here.
- **`gc/hb`, `gc/maintenance_state`, pool-algorithm registration, owner/catalog bootstrap and probe writes** were omitted. Their existing protections limit effects to leadership/reclaim progress or monotone/bootstrap state; I found no separate data-loss counterexample for them.

With plain/catalog admission retained, observer waiting cannot simply disappear. If R is the admission reserve and B bounds **admission-to-store-effect**, the idealized minimum wait after first observing a token is `L − R + B`, with clock-rate conversion. Thus it can be shorter than L when B < R. No finite wait proves safety against an unbounded admission-to-dispatch pause.

My verdict: the lease can become a liveness hint, but the report’s three patches are insufficient. The smallest defensible set includes Z6 and Z15, durable Z8 ownership validation, publication retry/incarnation repair, plain-file authority through immutable versions and existing refs, and a journal-authorized removal protocol. Preserve seals if permanent slot rejection is required; otherwise explicitly treat recreated old records as obsolete. Administrative retirement also needs closure if included in the guarantee. Existing wire schemas can support the plain-file redesign, but deployment needs migration; a strict freeze of current lookup semantics leaves time-based safety necessary. GC lane sealing remains optional.
